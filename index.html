2.5% of the field ran the second half faster

Every coach tells marathoners to negative split. I cut every finisher's 2014 Boston race in two at the halfway mat. Of 31,912 runners, 803 came home faster than they went out, and the median runner's second half was 14% slower than the first.

One square = 1% of finishers

The field

Nearly everyone slows down

The whole distribution of second-half slowdowns sits on the slow side of zero. Its peak is around 12 to 15%, and the median is 14%. That is the typical runner, so a handful of blow-ups can't explain it.

By finish time

The faster the runner, the smaller the fade

Runners who finished under 3 hours slowed by a median of 5.3% in the second half. Runners slower than 4 hours slowed by 21.7%, about four times as much. Fitter runners are further from their limit at halfway, so the pattern is an association and can't show that even pacing makes anyone faster.

Women slowed a little less than men: a median of 13.4% against 14.8%.

Method and limits

Official 2014 Boston results from the public llimllib/bostonmarathon mirror, 31,912 finishers with a valid half-marathon split. First half is the halfway mat time; second half is finish time minus that. Python with pandas and matplotlib; fetch_data.py then analyze.py reproduce every number.

One race on one course. Boston's early downhill and its hills between miles 16 and 21 make an even split harder than on a flat course, and its qualified field is faster than a typical marathon's. The halfway split is the only checkpoint used, and weather is not modeled.

The useful reading: almost nobody in this field started too slowly. The full writeup has the split medians for every finish band.
